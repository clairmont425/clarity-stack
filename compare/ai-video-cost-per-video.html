What a 30-second AI video actually costs

"Cheaper than a film crew" is true and useless. Here is the actual line-by-line API cost of one 30-second piece out of my pipeline, at list prices, with the arithmetic visible so you can re-run it on your own build.

Unit prices (list, checked 2026-11-02)

  • Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro image-to-video on fal: $0.35 for 5s, +$0.07 per extra second → a 5-second clip is $0.35. (source)
  • Gemini image generation, standard tier: $0.0336 at 1K, $0.0504 at 2K, $0.1130 at 4K. (source)
  • ElevenLabs narration: $0 marginal on an existing subscription — the plan is a fixed cost, so it doesn't belong in per-piece maths.
  • Edit, assembly, captions, 9:16 reframe: $0. FFmpeg and Python.

What the numbers actually tell you

  1. Video is 91% of the cost of a video. $2.10 of $2.3016. Every image in the piece together costs less than one 5-second clip. So iterate hard on stills — they're free by comparison — and only commit to motion once the frame is right.
  2. Retries are the real budget. At $0.35 a clip, three bad attempts on one shot costs more than every still in the project. The expensive mistake is generating motion from a still you weren't sure about.
  3. Volume is not the constraint people think it is. Thirty pieces of this kind costs under $70 in generation. Which is the uncomfortable finding: if output volume were the bottleneck, $70 would fix it. It never is. Distribution is.
  4. $10/week buys four 30-second pieces, or eight 15-second pieces, or 99 static images. A realistic mix — two 30s, one 15s, eight images — is $6.594, leaving headroom for the retries in point 2.

All figures are list price and exclude the fixed subscriptions underneath (ElevenLabs plan, storage, my time). They're a floor, not a quote.
