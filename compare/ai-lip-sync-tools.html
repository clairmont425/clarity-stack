Comparisons

Four ways to lip-sync an AI presenter. None of them shipped.

This is the piece of research I'd have paid for before starting. I needed one thing: a generated presenter whose mouth matches an ElevenLabs narration track, without wrecking the face. I tried four approaches on the same shot. I'm still not shipping a talking head.

Everything below comes out of production work, not a trial account opened to write a review. Where I have numbers, they were computed in code and I show the arithmetic. Where I don't, I say so instead of rounding a guess into a fact. How I test →

The test

Same source character, same script, same narration audio. One judgement call, made by eye on the rendered file: would I put this in front of a client? Not a benchmark score — a go/no-go, which is the only question that matters when you're the one delivering.

The pattern

Every tool trades one for the other. The models with the best lip sync generate the face themselves, and their faces are worse. The best face comes from a model that doesn't do audio, and bolting sync on afterwards looks bolted on. Nothing in this set gives you premium sync and preserved skin detail, and that's not a prompt-engineering gap — it's where the two families of model are architecturally.

What I do instead (and it's better content anyway)

I stopped treating a talking head as a requirement and designed the format so there isn't one:

  • Host appears, host doesn't speak on camera. She's in the hook and the close as picture; the narration runs over B-roll, screen capture and text.
  • The middle of the video is a real screen recording of the thing being discussed. No mouth, and far better proof than a face claiming something.
  • Text on screen carries the lines where a human would need lip movement.

The constraint improved the work. A mouth that doesn't match reads as fake and tanks trust; a clean screen recording with good narration reads as competent. I'd now choose this format even if lip sync were solved.

Still open

The gap in this test: I haven't personally run HeyGen, D-ID, Wav2Lip or SadTalker on the same shot. They're the obvious next candidates and I won't have an opinion worth reading until I do. When I run them, the results land on this page with the same go/no-go.
