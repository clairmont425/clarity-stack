How I test

Short version: everything here comes from tools running in actual production work, the numbers are computed rather than recalled, and I tell you when a claim is weak. If you only read one section, read the last one.

1. Production use, not trial accounts

I review tools I pay for and run weekly to deliver real work. The failures on this site — four lip-sync approaches, a warping t-shirt print — are failures that cost me project time. That's the only reason they're worth reading: a reviewer with a free trial and a deadline never finds them.

2. One judgement: would I deliver this?

No scores out of ten. A render either goes in front of a client or it doesn't. "Best sync of the four" and "unusable" are both true of the same tool, and a 7.5/10 would have hidden that.

3. Numbers are computed, never estimated

Every cost on this site was calculated from a vendor's current list price, in code, and I show the components so you can check the arithmetic. If I can't compute something I write "unknown" rather than a confident round number. Related: when an API won't let me read a figure — ElevenLabs credit balance, for one, thanks to a key-scope issue — I say the figure is unreadable instead of guessing at it.

4. Prices are dated

Every price says when I checked it and links the vendor's own page. AI pricing changes monthly. A review site that quotes a price with no date is telling you something it hasn't verified.

5. What I treat as weak evidence

  • Affiliate-aggregator commission figures. Sites listing "30% recurring!" are themselves monetised by those programs. I mark these "confirm in the dashboard" and never build a plan on them.
  • Income claims. "Faceless creators earn $5–15k/month" traces back, every time I followed it, to a vendor selling the tool. Nothing here depends on those numbers and you won't find them repeated.
  • My own single-shot tests. The lip-sync comparison is one character and one script. It's honest about what it is: enough to make a production decision, not a benchmark. Four tools, one shot each.
  • Specs I haven't held. The gear page says on the page that it's criteria, not hands-on testing.

6. Corrections

When something here turns out to be wrong — a price moves, a model improves, HeyGen solves the sync problem — I edit the page and note what changed, rather than quietly deleting it. The failures are the valuable part of the archive.
