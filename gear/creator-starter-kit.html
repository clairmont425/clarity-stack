Gear

Creator audio & lighting: what to look for

Read this first — it's a criteria page, not a product test. I've spent fifteen years in professional production, so I can tell you with confidence what specifications matter and why. I have not personally tested most current consumer creator gear, so you will not find me saying "I used this for six months" about a microphone I haven't owned. Where I name a category leader it's on published specification and professional judgement, and I say so. Anyone claiming hands-on experience of forty products in one article is lying to you, and you should assume the links are the reason.

The order that actually matters

Most people buy a camera first. That's backwards. Ranked by how much each dollar improves the finished piece:

  1. Audio. Viewers forgive a soft picture and leave over bad sound. It's not close.
  2. Light. A cheap sensor in good light beats an expensive sensor in bad light, every time.
  3. Camera. The phone in your pocket is, genuinely, enough to start.
  4. Everything else. Tripods, backdrops, teleprompters — real, but last.

Audio: the four specs worth checking

  • Polar pattern. Cardioid for one person in an untreated room — it rejects what's behind the mic, which is most of your room noise. Omni only if you're miking two people with one mic.
  • Dynamic vs condenser. In an untreated room, choose dynamic. A condenser is more sensitive and that sensitivity is spent on your refrigerator, your neighbours and your room's reflections. This single choice fixes more amateur audio than any plugin.
  • Connection. USB for simplicity; XLR if you ever want two mics or a real preamp. Don't buy XLR without budgeting for the interface.
  • Distance discipline. Free and worth more than any upgrade: get the mic within a hand's width of your mouth. Inverse square law does more for your signal-to-noise than spending another $200.

Room before gear. A duvet on the wall behind the camera, a rug, and closed curtains will beat a mic upgrade in a bare room. Reflections are the problem; a better mic hears them more clearly.

Light: the three specs worth checking

  • Size relative to your face. Big soft source = flattering. A bare panel the size of a paperback is a hard light no matter what it cost. Diffusion area is the spec.
  • CRI / TLCI. Look for CRI 95+. Cheap LEDs render skin green or magenta and no amount of grading fully recovers it.
  • Bicolour or RGB vs daylight-only. Only pay for tunable colour if you're genuinely mixing with window light. Otherwise it's a feature you'll set once.

And the free version: sit facing a window during daylight. That's a huge, soft, CRI-100 source you already own. I'd put off buying a key light until you've outgrown it.

Camera

Start with your phone, locked: manual exposure if available, 4K if you have the storage, 24 or 30fps consistently, and on a tripod. The jump from handheld phone to tripod-mounted phone is larger than the jump from phone to entry-level mirrorless. Upgrade when you have a specific limitation you can name — shallow depth of field, low light, interchangeable lenses — not before.
