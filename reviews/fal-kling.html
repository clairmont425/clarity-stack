fal.ai + Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro

VerdictKeep — with a hard boundary

The best face-preserving image-to-video I've paid for, and completely unusable for the one job I most wanted it for.

Worth it for
putting motion into a generated human still while keeping the face intact
Skip it if
anything with legible text, a logo or a printed graphic in frame

Why Kling, specifically

I compared it against the alternatives on the same source stills, judging one thing: does the face survive? Kling's did. Seedance 2.0 smoothed skin into a pasty, pore-free Instagram-filter face even with seven reference images. Veo 3.1 has the best native audio of the three and a face too cartoony to use. For face-forward work Kling is the only one of the three I'll run — the full side-by-side is in the lip-sync comparison.

Pricing: read the per-second model before you plan a project

Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro image-to-video on fal is quoted on the model page as $0.35 for a 5-second video, plus $0.07 for every additional second (fal model page, checked 2026-11-02).

That's $0.07/sec at the margin and $0.07/sec on average — a flat rate dressed up as a base fee. What it means in practice: length is cheap, retries are not. A 30-second piece built as six 5-second clips costs $2.10 in video. Three failed attempts at one of those clips costs you another $1.05 and buys nothing. Budget for the retries, not the runtime — the honest line-by-line build is on the cost page.

The failure that set a permanent rule

I needed a product video: a t-shirt with a printed graphic, with a gentle push-in. Every Kling run morphed the print — the artwork breathed, letters bent, the design drifted frame to frame. It wasn't a prompt problem. Generative video has no concept of "this region is a fixed asset and must stay pixel-exact".

So I stopped generating that shot and built it in code instead: resample the real print file into every frame against the garment photo. Pixel-exact at every zoom, zero dollars, deterministic. The whole reasoning is in when not to use AI video. The rule it left me with: generative video for faces and motion, code for anything that must stay exactly itself.

Notes on fal as a platform

  • Model-per-endpoint, pay-per-call, no seat fee — the right shape for pipeline work.
  • Endpoint paths are specific and version-sensitive; the multi-reference Seedance route, for instance, is a different path than the obvious image-to-video one. Read the model page rather than guessing a URL.
  • A 404 on a job ID means your key authenticated and the job simply isn't there. Useful signal when you're debugging auth: 404 is good news, 401 is the bad one.
  • No affiliate program I could find as of 2026-11-02, which is why this review has nothing to sell you.
