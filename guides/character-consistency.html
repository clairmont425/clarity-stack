Keeping one AI face consistent across a series

If you generate a recurring character, drift is the thing that will give you away. Not resolution, not motion — the fact that she's subtly a different woman in shot four. Here's the cause and the fix, both of which took me longer to find than they should have.

The cause, stated precisely

Drift happens when scene images are generated from text prompts instead of being anchored to a canonical image. "25-year-old blonde surfer, freckles, golden tan" is a description of a type, not a person. Run it twice and you get two different people who both match the description. Pile up a series that way and the face wanders — a little per shot, obviously by the end.

The fix

  1. Lock a canonical. One front-facing reference image, generated once, chosen carefully, never regenerated. This file is now the character's identity. Mine is literally canonical_front.png and everything points at it.
  2. Pass it as an image reference on every call. Not a text description of it — the actual file, as the model's image input. This requires a model that accepts reference images; it's the specific reason I use Gemini's reference-capable image model for anything with a face.
  3. Use image-to-image at roughly 0.3 strength rather than fresh text-to-image. Low enough to let pose, outfit and setting change; high enough that the face stays the same face. That number is where it stopped looking wrong for me — treat it as a starting point and nudge it for your character.
  4. Reuse scene references, regenerate the character pass. Lighting and composition anchors are reusable across characters; the identity pass isn't. This is also how you get a consistent look, not just a consistent face.
  5. Keep a rigid folder convention. Canonicals, face options, body options, turnaround references and per-scene references in fixed paths. Sounds like housekeeping; it's the thing that makes anchoring actually happen, because the right reference is always findable by a script instead of by memory.

What this costs

Onboarding a new character properly — four canonical options plus a seven-image turnaround set at 2K — came to $0.5544 at the list prices on the cost page. One-time, about fifty-five cents, and it is the highest-leverage spend in the whole pipeline. Skipping it to save that is how you end up re-rendering a series.

What's still unsolved

Anchoring hugely reduces drift; it doesn't end it. Big pose changes, strong directional light and extreme close-ups still pull the face. And drift is not the only consistency problem — the lip-sync models that flatten skin texture will undo a perfectly anchored face in a single pass. Consistency has to survive the whole chain, not just the image step.
